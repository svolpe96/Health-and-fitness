## Rules
Main lifts RPE 7–8 ceiling; secondary bench/squat and returning sumo may be 6–7 or lower, never force load to hit RPE. No grinders, failure, added sets or PRs. Accessories RPE 7–8, last set only. Rest 3–5 minutes main lifts and 1–3 minutes accessories. Loads are starting suggestions in lb, not completed results. Keep equipment/technique consistent. If first set exceeds 8 or technique degrades, reduce 2.5–5%; if still above cap, stop or remove remaining set. If warmups unusually hard or recovery poor, omit optional work and one accessory set per movement as needed. If two consecutive exposures deteriorate or soreness persists >48–72h, reduce one main set in affected lift and reassess deficit. If groin pain returns or worsens or changes mechanics, stop/modify squat or sumo; seek evaluation if persistent. Track during/after/next day. No makeup work.
## Progression
Week 1 calibrate. Weeks 2–3 repeat successful loads and reps; no obligatory increases. Only if all sets <=7, technique consistent and recovery stable across two exposures, consider +5 lb bench or +5–10 lb squat/sumo (sumo only if symptom-free); otherwise hold. Week 4 optional only if recovery and weight trend support it; reduce accessory sets further if needed or end cut. Compare same load/reps/equipment and RPE, not historical maxes. Log every main set RPE, accessory last-set RPE, morning weight, sleep, hydration 1–5, energy 1–5; add soreness and groin response. No caffeine or training time. Keep using shared raw CSV with actual dates only, and separate weekly checklists here.
## Nutrition and exit
At ~166 lb, 1.5 lb/week is ~0.9%/week, aggressive given previous diet/groin history. For maximal retention prefer ~1.0–1.25 lb/week (about 0.6–0.75%/week); athlete's requested 1.5 lb/week is an upper target, not a requirement. Start from actual maintenance and consider a ~400–500 kcal/day deficit if retention is the priority; adjust by measured trends, not a fixed calorie-per-pound prediction. Protein target 180 g/day (working range 170–190 g, ~2.25–2.52 g/kg at 166 lb), distributed across 4 meals of roughly 40–50 g; adequate dietary fat (~45–60 g/day), remaining calories carbohydrate, prioritize a fed training session and carbohydrate around training. Consistent hydration/sodium and 7–9 h sleep. Creatine monohydrate 3–5 g/day if already tolerated and medically appropriate; no loading needed; for competition use third-party tested products. Track daily morning weight and 7-day averages; no calorie prescription without current intake/maintenance data. If persistent strength decline, low energy, dizziness, or recurrent injury, reduce deficit or stop cut. No dehydration. Return to maintenance calories after 3–4 weeks; first week hold cut loads, restore ~half removed accessory sets; second week restore remaining sets if RPE/recovery stable, then resume progression in program/current with a new week based on current data rather than blindly repeating old prescriptions.

## Evidence update — November 26, 2026
- ACSM 2026 resistance training position stand: heavy loads support strength; failure is not necessary. General healthy adult evidence, not a mini-cut-specific trial. https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000003897
- Refalo, Trexler & Helms 2026: 29-study meta-regression in resistance-trained people dieting suggests higher protein is associated with more favorable fat-free mass change, particularly leaner participants; heterogeneity limits precise dosing claims. https://doi.org/10.1519/SSC.0000000000000888
- ISSN 2026 body-weight reduction stand supports higher protein in lean trained individuals in a deficit. https://doi.org/10.1080/15502783.2026.2740266
- Roth et al. 2023: moderate vs high volume during energy restriction in trained males did not show a lean mass preservation advantage for higher volume. https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.14237
- 2026 four-week trial: 12 vs 30 sets per muscle group/week with high protein and caloric restriction; no greater fat-free mass sparing with higher volume, though higher-volume group had strength benefits. This does not establish a universal minimum or optimal volume. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41430-026-01757-8
- Murphy & Koehler 2022: energy deficiency impaired lean mass gains more than strength gains; ~500 kcal/day prevented lean mass gains in meta-regression, not a hard threshold for muscle loss. https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.14075
- Garthe et al. 2011: slower weight reduction had more favorable lean mass and strength outcomes in athletes. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21558571/
- Carbohydrate performance review: benefits are context dependent (fasted, longer/higher-volume training). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35215506/
- Creatine and resistance training strength meta-analysis: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11547435/
### Application
Keep all four existing weekly prescriptions intact initially; 44 core sets already represent a moderate reduction against full Week 8 prescription. Do not cut volume automatically again or add heavy singles; maintain familiar loads, main-lift RPE caps and productive accessory sets, with autoregulation. Compare same lift, load, reps, equipment and RPE. If same-load RPE rises roughly 1+ on two comparable exposures or performance falls ~5% persistently, review sleep, carbs, symptoms, weight trend and deficit; skip optional Saturday first, reduce redundant accessory sets if needed, and reduce deficit rather than repeatedly lowering training stimulus. These are coaching decision thresholds, not validated cutoffs. After first-week water shifts, if rolling weight loss exceeds ~1.5 lb/week or recovery deteriorates, increase calories by ~150–250/day and reassess over a week. End the deficit early if persistent deterioration despite adjustments or recurrent injury. Do not interpret glycogen/water-related changes in scale weight, DXA lean mass or gym performance as proof of muscle loss.
